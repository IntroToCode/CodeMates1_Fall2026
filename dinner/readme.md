# Kebbeh bil Sanieh (Lebanese Baked Kebbeh Tray)

## Description

Kebbeh bil Sanieh is the baked tray version of Lebanon's national dish, a bulgur and lamb shell layered over a spiced filling of onion and toasted pine nuts, scored into diamonds before it goes in the oven. The technique of pounding cracked wheat with meat in a stone mortar is an old Levantine village practice shared across Lebanon, Syria, and Iraq, and this tray form is what households made when they needed to feed a crowd from one pan rather than shaping dozens of individual torpedoes.

## Spice Level: - Not spicy at all, focusing on the savory flavors dominating the dish.

### Ingredients:

**For the shell**
- [ ] 1000g fine bulgur (#1 grade)
- [ ] 1400g lean ground lamb, from leg or shoulder
- [ ] 2 medium onion, quartered
- [ ] 3 tsp ground allspice
- [ ] 2 tsp ground cinnamon
- [ ] 1 tsp black pepper
- [ ] 4 tsp salt
- [ ] 240ml ice water

**For the filling**
- [ ] 800g ground lamb, roughly 20% fat
- [ ] 4 medium onions, finely diced
- [ ] 160g pine nuts
- [ ] 6 tbsp ghee or clarified butter
- [ ] 2 tsp ground allspice
- [ ] 1 tsp ground cinnamon
- [ ] 2 tsp salt
- [ ] 4 tbsp pomegranate molasses

**For the tray**
- [ ] 160ml olive oil, for greasing and drizzling

### Steps for preparation:

1. Rinse the fine bulgur in a sieve under cold running water, then transfer it to a bowl, cover with cold water, and leave it to soften for 15 minutes. Drain it in the sieve and then squeeze it firmly between your hands to force out the water. It should feel damp and springy, never wet.
2. While the bulgur soaks, melt the ghee in a wide pan over medium heat. Add the diced onions and cook for about 10 minutes, stirring now and then, until they are soft and lightly golden.
3. Add the fattier ground lamb to the onions, breaking it apart with the back of a spoon so it browns evenly rather than clumping. Cook until no pink remains, roughly 6 to 8 minutes.
4. Stir the allspice, cinnamon, salt, and pomegranate molasses into the meat. Cook for 2 more minutes so the spices bloom, then take the pan off the heat.
5. In a separate dry pan over low heat, toast the pine nuts, shaking the pan constantly. They go from pale to golden in under 3 minutes and burn within seconds of that, so pull them off while they are still lighter than you want. Stir them into the filling and set it aside to cool completely.
6. Make the shell. Put the quartered onion in a food processor and blitz it to a paste. Add the lean lamb, allspice, cinnamon, black pepper, and salt, and process until the mixture is smooth and almost pasty.
7. Add the squeezed bulgur to the processor in three batches, pulsing between each and adding the ice water one spoonful at a time. Stop when the mixture is a smooth, slightly tacky dough that holds its shape when you press a piece between your fingers. Do not let it turn wet.
8. Heat the oven to 200C. Brush a 33 x 23 cm baking tray with some of the olive oil.
9. Set a bowl of cold water next to the tray and wet your hands. Take half the shell mixture and press it evenly across the base of the tray in a layer about 1cm thick, re-wetting your hands whenever the dough starts to stick.
10. Spread the cooled filling over the base in a level layer, leaving a 1cm border clear around the edge so the two shell layers can seal together.
11. Take golf-ball-sized pieces of the remaining shell mixture and flatten each one between wet palms into a thin patty. Lay the patties over the filling like tiles, overlapping slightly, then smooth the seams with wet fingers until the top is one continuous unbroken sheet.
12. Dip a knife in water and cut diagonal lines across the tray in both directions to form diamonds, pressing all the way down to the base. Run a wet finger around the inside edge of the tray to loosen the sides.
13. Press your thumb into the centre of each diamond to make a shallow well, then drizzle the remaining olive oil generously over the whole surface, letting it pool into the scored lines and the wells.
14. Bake for 40 to 45 minutes, until the top is deep golden brown and the kebbeh has visibly pulled away from the sides of the tray. If the colour is still pale at 45 minutes, finish it under the grill for 2 to 3 minutes, watching it closely.
15. Rest the tray for 10 minutes before serving, then cut through the scored lines and lift the diamonds out with a spatula. Serve warm or at room temperature.

### Notes:

**Bulgur grade is the one thing you cannot substitute.** Fine bulgur (#1) hydrates into a workable dough. Medium or coarse bulgur stays gritty and the shell will crumble the moment you try to lift a piece out of the tray. Check the number on the packet.

**Keep your hands wet.** The shell dough is sticky by design, and a dry palm will tear the layer you just smoothed. A bowl of cold water next to the tray is standard practice in Lebanese kitchens and makes the assembly far less frustrating.

**Serving.** Traditionally eaten with cucumber and mint yoghurt (*laban bi khyar*), a sharp fattoush, and raw onion wedges. Leftovers are eaten at room temperature the next day, which many Lebanese households actively prefer to the hot version.

**Substitutions.** Ground beef works in place of lamb and is common in diaspora kitchens, though the flavour is noticeably flatter. For the Lenten vegetarian version eaten in Christian villages, replace the shell meat with 400g of cooked and mashed potato, and fill with spiced chickpeas and caramelised onion.

**Make ahead.** Assemble the full tray, cover it tightly, and refrigerate for up to 24 hours. Bake it straight from cold and add 10 minutes. It also freezes raw, well wrapped, for up to two months. Bake from frozen at 180C for about 70 minutes.

# Drink Pairings!

**Arak (Alcoholic Option)**
Arak is Lebanon’s signature spirit, a clear anise-flavored drink distilled from grapes. When water is added it turns milky white, which is why it is nicknamed “the milk of lions.” Its licorice-like aroma cuts through the richness of fried Kebbeh, so it is the classic choice for a traditional meal.
Ingredients (Per Serving)
	• Arak: 50 ml
	• Water (chilled): 100 ml, roughly two parts water to one part arak
	• Ice cubes: As needed
	• Optional garnish: A small sprig of fresh mint
Method
	1. Pour the arak into a glass first.
	2. Add the chilled water slowly and watch it turn cloudy.
	3. Add ice last, then stir gently and serve right away.

**Jallab (Non-Alcoholic Option)**
This drink is a traditional Levantine fruit syrup drink made by diluting date molasses (or grape/carob molasses) and rose water with water, often infused with a wisp of Arabic incense, and served ice-cold. It complements the dish well because it is non-alcoholic and brings a stronger flavor to the Kebbeh recipe.
Ingredients (Per Serving)
	• Date molasses (dibs) or grape molasses: 3 tablespoons (about 45 ml)
	• Rose water: 1 teaspoon (or to taste)
	• Water (chilled): 1 cup (235 ml)
	• Crushed ice: As needed
	• Garnish: Toasted or soaked pine nuts and golden raisins (sultanas)
	• Optional smoky touch: A tiny whiff of natural Arabic incense (such as frankincense or myrrh) smoked inside the serving glass or pitcher
